How it works

Proof of Stake, with one deliberate difference

Instead of miners burning electricity, people who hold FIC run a node and take turns producing the blocks that record transactions — the network picks who produces each block at random, weighted by how many coins they've put forward. That part is ordinary Proof-of-Stake.

What's different is the reward. Whoever produces a block receives a fixed 10 FIC, plus that block's transaction fees — never more, never less, and never scaled by the size or age of the stake that won it. A larger stake wins blocks more often, in proportion to its size; it does not earn more per block. That distinction is the whole reason the reward model looks the way it does — read why on the Shariah design notes page.

Where things actually stand

What we won't quietly leave out

A lot of crypto projects blur the line between "built" and "planned." This one draws it plainly:

Testnet, not mainnet

FirstIslamicCoin is currently a public testnet. Mainnet launches only after a genesis key ceremony and a security review — see the roadmap. There is no live mainnet, no price, and no exchange listing.

No wallets shipped yet

The desktop node builds; mobile, web, and packaged desktop wallets are still in development. The wallets page says exactly what exists today and what doesn't.

No Shariah endorsement

"Shariah-conscious" describes design intent, not a certification. No scholar or advisory board has reviewed or endorsed this project. The design notes say this directly and will keep saying it until that changes.
